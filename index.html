Why product?

        
For years, my job was making fast, defensible decisions with incomplete information: on patrol, training new officers, and leading soldiers. Product management uses the same muscle. Understand the people you serve, define the real problem, and deliver something that works under pressure.

        
"From the street to the sprint: same mission, new terrain."

        
Strategic Business Readiness

        
Military strategy and business strategy use the same categories. Only the jargon changes. Commander's intent becomes the product vision, mission analysis becomes market and problem analysis, and wargaming courses of action becomes comparing strategic options before committing resources. Resource and logistics planning maps to budgets and operations, the 3 M's (Mission, Men, Me) set my priorities, and every after action review becomes a retro that makes the next plan better.

      

    

  
  


  

  
  

    

      

        
02 / FAQ
Questions I get

        
Straight answers about a non-traditional path into product.

      

      

        
How does law enforcement translate to product?
Both jobs mean understanding people under pressure, prioritizing with limited time, and documenting decisions early. Every call is like a stakeholder meeting: I assess the situation and clarify what people need and how I can provide it. Knowing my team and the resources at hand is crucial to delivering what the client or stakeholder needs. The real skill is reading between the lines to identify the pain points and blockers they need removed.

        
How do you approach business strategy?
I start with the mission: what outcome the business needs and who it serves. Then I look at the people it affects, the market and competitors, and what it will cost in time and resources. I would rather validate an idea cheaply before committing to it. The goal is a clear plan the team understands and can carry out without waiting on me.
For example, before an 11-day, multi-agency event, I walked the grounds with a fire department battalion chief and the RAAM program's officer in charge. The topographic maps we had planned from did not show the true size of the site or the extra routes in and out. I brought that data to event leadership, they approved the change, and we moved from one forward control point to three. That was my lesson in strategy in motion, and I carry it into business: a plan is a starting point. When the ground truth changes, the strategy has to change with it.

        
Do you have remote work experience?
Yes. I work remotely as a contractor from a dedicated home office, and I'm very comfortable in a remote setting.

        
What kind of company are you looking for?
Small to mid-size tech teams where I can learn fast and own real outcomes.
